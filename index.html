Background

I'm a systems neuroscientist investigating the neural mechanisms of learning, memory, and cognitive flexibility in rodents. My work asks how the brain integrates information across distributed circuits to form and update memories in response to changing reward contingencies.

My doctoral research at Brandeis University (Jadhav Lab) established that VTA dopamine neurons, mPFC ensembles, and CA1 populations switch their representations simultaneously during rule learning — providing a mechanistic account of how reward signals and cognitive representations are coordinated for behavioral flexibility.

Earlier work with Watson and Buzsáki characterized the fundamental relationship between local field potentials and spike rates across neocortex — a methodological foundation for LFP interpretation in vivo.

// research focus

Research Projects

Temporal coordination of DA, mPFC, and CA1 during rule switching
mPFC · CA1 · VTA

Using simultaneous multi-tetrode recordings from CA1, mPFC, and optogenetically photo-tagged VTA dopamine neurons in TH-Cre rats, I showed that rule-specific predictive DA activity develops simultaneously with correct rule decoding in mPFC and CA1 within a few trials after an unsignaled rule switch — establishing a circuit-level mechanism for dopamine-driven cognitive flexibility.

Rule and spatial representations in mPFC and hippocampal CA1
Prefrontal · Hippocampus

mPFC and CA1 ensemble activity encodes abstract task rules during a novel W-track rule-switching paradigm. Single cells and population codes in both regions remap on common spatial trajectories when the governing rule changes — revealing coordinated representational updating tracked via population decoding across the course of learning.

Spike–LFP coupling across frequency bands in neocortex
LFP · Neocortex · Methods

In collaboration with Watson and Buzsáki, I characterized how LFP power in different frequency bands relates to spiking in rat neocortex. High-frequency oscillations (50–180 Hz) are the most reliable proxy for local firing, but spiking itself and EMG contribute directly to broadband LFP power — motivating caution in interpreting such signals as purely oscillatory.

Ventral tegmental area dopamine neural activity switches simultaneously with rule representations in the medial prefrontal cortex and hippocampus
Ding, M., Tomsick, P. L., Young, R. A., Jadhav, S. P.
Journal of Neuroscience, 45(37): e1670242025 · 2025
Simultaneous recordings from CA1, mPFC, and optogenetically identified VTA dopamine neurons during a W-track rule-switching task revealed that DA reward-predictive coding and prefrontal/hippocampal rule representations update in a coordinated, near-simultaneous manner — establishing a circuit-level mechanism for dopamine-driven cognitive flexibility.

Temporal coupling of field potentials and action potentials in the neocortex
Watson, B. O., Ding, M., Buzsáki, G.
European Journal of Neuroscience, 48(7): 2482–2497 · 2018
Systematic characterization of spike–LFP relationships across frequency bands in rat neocortex. High-frequency power (50–180 Hz) is the most reliable proxy for local spiking; spiking and EMG contribute directly to broadband LFP power; and an improved firing-rate predictor is derived using the full LFP spectrum.
